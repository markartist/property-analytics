# Captain Command Hierarchy

Status: Draft v1
Date: 04/28/2026
Owner: MarketingOps / Property Analytics
Scope: Role language for Captain's Log, Captain's Brief, support agents, and executive escalation

## Purpose

The Captain system needs memorable role language without turning the product into theater.

This hierarchy defines the operating vocabulary for property intelligence, memory, source ownership, escalation, and executive readouts.

Use these names to clarify responsibility, audience, and escalation level. Do not use the metaphor to obscure source authority, human accountability, or Data Pond truth.

## Primary Roles

| Role | Plain Meaning | System Responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fleet Commander | Senior business owner / system sponsor | Defines operating doctrine, priorities, and acceptable decision posture across the portfolio |
| Chief of Staff | System orchestrator / strategic AI collaborator | Coordinates design, doctrine, workflow, agent structure, and cross-system execution on behalf of Fleet Command |
| Admiral | VP or senior operating leader | Receives Captain readouts, makes regional/property recovery decisions, and resolves escalations |
| Commodore | Portfolio or regional operating leader | Oversees multiple Captains, identifies cross-property patterns, and promotes local lessons to fleet guidance |
| Fleet Scribe | Official report and archive office | Assembles approved report artifacts from Captain, Commodore, Fleet, and expert-bench inputs; owns templates, delivery, repository placement, archive hygiene, version discipline, and source panels |
| Captain | Property-scoped intelligence owner | Owns the Captain's Log, reconciles the property's evidence set, commands the support team, holds each role accountable for its lane, and delivers the Captain's Brief |
| First Officer | Human property/regional operator paired with the Captain | Executes field actions, validates local context, and confirms operational reality |

## Captain Command Responsibility

The Captain is accountable for the whole property intelligence team doing its job.

The Captain does not personally execute every specialist task, but the Captain must:

- know which support roles are assigned
- know the current status of each support lane
- detect when a role has not reported, has stale evidence, or has produced an incomplete read
- challenge weak or generic recommendations
- reconcile conflicts between specialist lanes
- convert specialist findings into one coherent property directive
- assign or escalate follow-up through the Boatswain, First Officer, Commodore, or Admiral
- remember whether prior specialist recommendations worked

Support agents are specialists. The Captain owns orchestration, judgment, and accountability.

## Fleet Scribe Responsibility

The Fleet Scribe is the official publication and archive owner.

The Fleet Scribe must:

- use the approved report-family template and delivery channel
- preserve approved structure, labels, section order, and audience boundary
- assemble the final artifact from Captain, Commodore, Fleet, Data Pond, and expert-bench inputs
- keep document, spreadsheet, JSON, email, repository, and delivery-log outputs aligned
- enforce MM/DD/YYYY date formatting and brand/header rules
- place artifacts in the shared repository and maintain archive hygiene
- stop publication when the approved format and generated output materially differ

The Fleet Scribe must not:

- invent property conclusions
- override Data Pond source truth
- redesign an approved executive artifact without explicit approval
- let expert-bench consultation become a competing report

## Language Rules

1. Use the role names to make ownership clear.
2. Keep external-facing or executive-facing language plain when the metaphor would distract.
3. Keep `Captain` visible in product and report language because it identifies the property owner role.
4. Use `Admiral` as the executive recipient role when the audience understands the model; otherwise render it as `VP Read` or `Executive Read`.
5. Never let role language replace evidence, source authority, or named human accountability.

## Boundary With Data Pond

The hierarchy does not create new truth ownership.

Data Pond remains the source of internal operational truth where a governed source exists. The Captain and support roles interpret, reconcile, escalate, and record. They do not overwrite source-of-record facts.
